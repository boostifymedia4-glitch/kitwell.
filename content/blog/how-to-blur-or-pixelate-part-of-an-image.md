Pictures and screenshots often show more than you mean to share. A street photo includes a licence plate. A screenshot of a chat shows a phone number. A picture of a parcel shows an address, and a photo of a document shows an ID number. Before you post, sell, report or send such a picture, the sensitive part has to be hidden.

This guide shows how to hide parts of a picture in your browser, explains the difference between blur, pixelation and a black box, and lists the other places where private information can hide.

:::takeaways
- Blur and pixelation can sometimes be partly reversed. For ID numbers, licence plates and other secrets, use the black box.
- You draw the boxes yourself, because the tool does not detect faces automatically.
- Cover a little more than the sensitive area, and zoom in to check the result.
- Your original file is never changed. The hidden version is saved as a new picture.
- Check the file name, the caption and the rest of the picture too, not only the part you covered.
:::

## When you need to hide part of a picture

The common cases are easy to recognise:

- Faces of people who did not agree to be shown, especially children.
- Licence plates in photos of cars for sale or of street scenes.
- Names, addresses and phone numbers in screenshots.
- Account numbers, order numbers and barcodes on receipts and parcels.
- Documents such as ID cards, tickets and letters.
- Private messages and notifications that appear at the edge of a screenshot.

In all of these cases the best habit is to hide the detail before the picture leaves your device, and to keep the original in a private place.

## Blur, pixelate or black box

<tool_blur_image_area>Blur or Pixelate Image</tool_blur_image_area> offers three ways to hide an area.

1. Blur smooths the area so details cannot be read at a glance. It looks natural and is good for faces in the background.
2. Pixelate turns the area into large coloured squares. It is a familiar look, and the squares are bigger the stronger you set the effect.
3. Black box paints the area solid black. Nothing remains of the original pixels in that area.

The difference matters for safety. Blur and pixelation keep a rough trace of the original, and with effort and software they can sometimes be partly reversed, especially when the text was large and clear. For anything that must stay private, such as an ID number, a licence plate, an address or a password, choose the black box.

## How to hide an area

The tool works in your browser tab, so the picture stays on your device.

1. Open the tool and add your picture. It accepts one image at a time, up to 25 MB.
2. Drag on the picture to draw a box over what you want to hide, or press the button to add a box and then move and resize it with the X, Y, width and height fields.
3. Choose blur, pixelate or black box. For blur and pixelate you can also set the strength.
4. Apply the effect and download the result.

You can draw as many boxes as you need. Before you apply, you can remove a box or redraw it, and your original file is not touched at any point.

## Drawing accurate boxes

Accuracy is what makes the difference between a safe picture and a leak.

- Make the box a little larger than the detail. A box that only just fits can leave the edge of a letter or the tip of a nose visible.
- For text, cover every character, including the first and the last.
- For a face, cover the whole head including the hair and ears if the person could be recognised from them.
- Zoom in on the finished picture and look at every edge.

If one box leaves a gap, add a second box that overlaps it. Overlapping boxes are fine.

## Check the whole picture, not only the part you covered

Private information hides in unexpected places.

- The picture can show a reflection in a window, a mirror or a pair of sunglasses.
- A screenshot can show a name in a tab, a notification or a status bar.
- A document photo can show text on the next page.
- A parcel can have a barcode that holds the address even if the printed address is covered.

Then think about everything that travels with the picture: the file name, the caption you write and the place where you post it. ## Limits of the tool

The tool is made for careful, manual work. It does not find faces automatically, because that needs a large AI model that is not included. It works on one picture at a time, and animated pictures use their first frame. If you have many pictures, take a moment to plan: a repeated detail such as a logo or a number can also be hidden one picture at a time.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Using blur or pixelation for a number or a name that must stay private.
- Drawing a box that only just fits the detail.
- Forgetting a second copy of the detail somewhere else in the picture.
- Leaving a revealing file name or caption.
- Sharing the original instead of the hidden version.
- Not zooming in to check the edges.

## A quick checklist

1. Find every sensitive detail in the picture, including reflections and edges.
2. Choose the black box for secrets, and blur for casual cases.
3. Draw boxes a little larger than the detail.
4. Apply, zoom in and check every edge.
5. Check the file name and caption, then share the new picture and keep the original private.

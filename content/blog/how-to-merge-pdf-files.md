Applications, tax returns, invoices, project reports: sooner or later somebody asks you for “everything in one PDF”. You have the cover letter in one file, the form in another, a scan of an ID in a third, and no wish to send three attachments that might be opened in the wrong order.

Merging fixes that. This guide shows how to combine PDFs in your browser, how to get the order right the first time, what merging does not carry over, and how to keep the final file small enough to send.

:::takeaways
- Merge PDF joins two or more PDFs into one document, in the order you set with the arrows.
- The files are combined on your own device and are not uploaded.
- Page content and links are kept; bookmarks and form fields of the source files are not.
- Password-protected PDFs must be unlocked first, and the merged file is best compressed once at the end.
:::

## When merging is the right move

Merge when the recipient needs a single document to read, print or archive: a job application pack, a set of supporting documents for a visa or loan, the monthly invoices for your accountant, or chapters of a report written by different people. A single PDF opens in one window, prints in one job and cannot lose a page on the way.

Do not merge when the files serve different purposes or will be edited separately. If one part of the pack changes often, keep it as its own file and merge only at the moment you send it.

## How to merge PDFs in your browser

<tool_merge_pdf>Merge PDF</tool_merge_pdf> runs inside your browser tab. Your documents are read from your device, joined locally and offered back as a download.

1. Open the tool and add your PDFs. You can choose several files at once or drag them onto the page. Each file can be up to 100 MB.
2. Put the files in the order you want. Use the arrows to move a file up or down. The list is the order of the final document.
3. Press Merge and wait a moment. Larger files take longer because your own device does the work.
4. Download the combined PDF and open it before you send it.

## Getting the order right

The most common mistake is not the merge itself but the order. The files are joined exactly as listed, so check the list from top to bottom before you press the button. A few habits help:

- Name your files so they sort naturally, for example 01-cover-letter, 02-form, 03-id-scan. When the names already carry the order, the list is right from the start.
- Put the page that explains the pack first. A short cover page or summary makes a long pack much easier to read.
- Check the page count afterwards. If three files of 2, 4 and 3 pages gave you 9 pages, the merge is complete.

If you need to change the order of individual pages after merging, <tool_reorder_pdf_pages>Reorder PDF Pages</tool_reorder_pdf_pages> shows thumbnails you can drag, and <tool_remove_pdf_pages>Remove PDF Pages</tool_remove_pdf_pages> deletes pages you do not need.

## What merging keeps and what it does not

Merging copies the visible content of every page: text, images, vector graphics and links. The merged file is a normal PDF that opens in any reader.

Some document-level features do not carry over from the source files:

- Bookmarks and outlines of the individual files are not merged. The combined file has no table of contents unless you build one elsewhere.
- Form fields are not merged, so a fillable form will be flattened into what it looks like. If a form must stay fillable, complete it before merging or send it as its own file.
- Password-protected PDFs are not supported. If one of your files asks for a password, remove it first with <tool_unlock_pdf>Unlock PDF</tool_unlock_pdf>, which works when you know the password.

> Tip: merge a copy, not your only version. Keep the originals in a folder so you can rebuild the pack if a page turns out to be missing or in the wrong place.

## Keep the merged file a sensible size

A merged PDF is as large as the sum of its parts, and attachment limits are set by the receiving system. Many email services accept attachments of roughly 20 to 25 MB in total, and online portals often allow much less.

If the result is too large, reduce it in this order:

1. Remove pages nobody needs, such as blank pages and duplicates.
2. Compress the merged file once with <tool_compress_pdf>Compress PDF</tool_compress_pdf>. Our guide to <post_compress-pdf-without-losing-quality>compressing a PDF without losing quality</post_compress-pdf-without-losing-quality> explains the strengths and what to expect.
3. Compress before you merge only if a single source file is huge, for example a scan with high-resolution photos.

Compressing the combined file once is better than compressing every part, because each round of recompression costs a little image quality.

## Add page numbers after merging

Each source file starts at page 1 on its own, so a merged pack can have several “page 1” labels printed on its pages. If readers will refer to pages by number, number the whole document after merging with <tool_add_page_numbers>Add Page Numbers</tool_add_page_numbers>, so the numbers run from the first page to the last.

## Merging scanned documents

Scans merge exactly like any other PDF, but remember two things. They are heavy, so check the size afterwards. And they contain pictures of text, not text, so the merged file will not be searchable. If you need to search it, add a text layer with OCR PDF; our guide to <post_ocr-scanned-pdf-searchable>making a scanned PDF searchable</post_ocr-scanned-pdf-searchable> shows how. If your starting point is a pile of photos, <post_convert-images-to-pdf>convert the images to PDF</post_convert-images-to-pdf> first and then merge the result with the rest.

## Merging and splitting go together

Merging is half of a pair. If a pack arrives as one large file and you only need parts of it, our guide to <post_how-to-split-a-pdf>splitting a PDF</post_how-to-split-a-pdf> shows how to cut it by range, page or chunk. A common routine is to split a document, replace one part, and merge the parts again.

## Fixing problems

- A file will not add: it is probably password-protected, damaged or over 100 MB. Unlock it, split it, or repair it first; our guide to <post_repair-a-corrupted-pdf>repairing a corrupted PDF</post_repair-a-corrupted-pdf> explains what can be recovered.
- The order is wrong after merging: go back, fix the list order and merge again from your originals. For a small fix, use Reorder PDF Pages.
- The merged file looks larger than expected: that is normal. Compress it once at the end.
- A fillable form no longer fills: form fields are not carried over by merging. Complete the form first, then merge.

:::faq Frequently asked questions
Q: Is it safe to merge confidential PDFs online?
A: Merge PDF runs in your browser, so the files are processed on your device and are not uploaded to a server. As with any sensitive document, avoid doing it on a shared or public computer.

Q: Is there a limit on how many files I can merge?
A: Each file can be up to 100 MB, and the tool limits how many files you can add at once to keep your browser responsive. For a very large pack, merge it in two stages.

Q: Will merging reduce the quality of my pages?
A: No. Pages are copied across without being recompressed, so text and images look the same. Quality only drops if you compress the merged file afterwards.

Q: Can I merge a PDF with images?
A: First turn the images into a PDF with Images to PDF, then merge that PDF with the others. That also lets you choose the page size and margins for the pictures.

Q: Can I undo a merge?
A: Split PDF can separate the combined file again by page ranges, but it is easier to keep your original files and simply merge again if you need a different version.
:::

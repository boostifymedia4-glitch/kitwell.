A watermark is a name, a logo or a short line of text placed on a picture. Photographers use it to show who made the picture, shops use it on product photos so that others do not pass the photos off as theirs, and designers use it on previews that are not yet paid for.

A watermark is not a lock, and it does not stop a determined person. Its job is to credit the author and to discourage casual copying. This guide shows how to add a watermark to a whole set of photos in your browser, how to design one that looks good, and what it can and cannot do.

:::takeaways
- You can stamp text or a logo on many pictures at once and download the results or a ZIP.
- A single mark in a corner is discreet. A tiled pattern is harder to remove but more intrusive.
- Choose the opacity and size so the mark is visible but does not spoil the picture.
- Marked copies are saved as new files, so your originals stay clean.
- A watermark deters casual copying only. It can be cropped or edited out.
:::

## Why add a watermark

There are three common reasons.

1. Credit. A small name or website address on a photo tells viewers who made it, even when the picture is shared far away from the original page.
2. Protection of previews. A strong tiled mark on a preview makes the picture useless for final use, until the buyer receives the clean version.
3. Branding. Product photos with a consistent logo look like they belong together.

Think about which of these you need, because it decides the style. Credit calls for a small, quiet mark. Protecting a preview calls for a large, repeated mark.

## Position and layout

You can place a single mark or repeat it in a tiled pattern.

A single mark can go in a corner or in the centre. Corners are discreet and the usual choice for credit. Keep the mark away from the main subject, but not so close to the edge that it could be cropped off in one move. A mark across the centre is more difficult to remove, and it is more noticeable.

A tiled layout repeats the mark across the whole picture. It is the best choice for previews and proofs, because there is no clean area to crop. For finished pictures it is usually too heavy, since viewers can no longer enjoy the photo.

## Size and opacity

Two settings decide how the watermark looks.

- Size. A mark that is too small can be missed, and one that is too large takes over. A good starting point for a corner mark is around a tenth to a fifth of the picture width, and you can adjust from there.
- Opacity. Reducing opacity lets the picture show through. Around 40 to 60 percent is a common choice for corner marks, and tiled marks are often fainter.

Always preview on a bright photo and a dark photo. A white mark that is clear on a dark picture can disappear on a bright sky, and a dark mark can do the opposite. If your set has both, choose a mid-tone colour or a mark with a thin outline.

## What a watermark does not do

Be realistic about what a watermark can achieve.

- It does not stop copying. Anyone can screenshot or download the picture.
- A corner mark can be cropped away, and a mark on a plain area can be painted over with editing software.
- It does not prove ownership by itself. Keep your original files, with their original dates, as evidence.
- Pictures lose their metadata when they are saved again here, so the details that identify the camera and the place are not carried over. ## Hiding what should not be shared at all

A watermark labels a picture; it does not hide anything in it. If a photo contains a licence plate, a face or an address that should not be shown, hide that part first. ## Common mistakes to avoid

- Making the mark so strong that nobody wants to look at the picture.
- Putting the mark in a corner that can be cropped off in seconds.
- Watermarking before resizing, so the mark ends up too small.
- Choosing a colour that disappears on some of your pictures.
- Deleting the clean originals.

## A quick checklist

1. Edit, crop and resize the pictures first.
2. Choose text for speed or a transparent PNG logo for a clean look.
3. Pick a single mark for credit, or a tiled pattern for previews.
4. Set the opacity and size, and test on a bright and a dark picture.
5. Download the marked copies and keep the clean masters.

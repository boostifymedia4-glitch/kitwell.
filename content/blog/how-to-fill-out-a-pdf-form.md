Applications, tax declarations, insurance claims, school registrations: a lot of paperwork still arrives as a PDF, and the first instinct is to print it, fill it in by hand and scan it again. That is slow, the handwriting is rarely tidy and the scan is large. Filling the form on screen is faster and produces a cleaner document.

This guide explains how to fill in a PDF form in your browser, what the difference is between editable and flattened results, and what to do when the PDF turns out not to be a real form at all.

:::takeaways
- Fillable PDFs contain real form fields: text boxes, checkboxes, radio buttons and drop-down menus.
- You can keep the filled form editable, or flatten it so the answers become part of the page.
- A PDF without fields, such as a scan, can still be typed onto by clicking the page where the text should go.
- XFA forms, used by some government and bank forms, cannot have their fields filled here.
- Check every page of the filled document before you send it.
:::

## Fillable forms versus flat documents

Not every PDF that looks like a form is one. There are three situations:

- A fillable form has real fields. When you open it, you can click a box and type. This is the best case.
- A flat form is only a picture or plain page. It looks like a form, but there is nothing to click, because the boxes are just drawn lines. Scans of paper forms are flat.
- An XFA form is a special dynamic form, built with an older Adobe technology. Some government and bank forms use it. Many tools cannot fill its fields.

## How to fill a form in your browser

The tool runs inside your browser tab, so the PDF is read from your device.

1. Open the tool and add the PDF (up to 100 MB). Unlock it first if it has a password.
2. If the form has fields, they are listed under the file name, grouped by page. Fill them in: type into text boxes, tick checkboxes, choose from menus.
3. Decide whether to flatten the form.
4. Save and download the filled PDF.

Fields marked as required show an asterisk, read-only fields are shown but cannot be changed, and the count under the list tells you how many fillable fields were found.

## Editable or flattened?

After filling, you choose how the answers are stored:

- Keep editable: the fields stay live. You or the recipient can change an answer later. Use this for drafts and for files you still need to correct.
- Flatten: the answers are burned into the page and the fields are removed, so nobody can edit them. Use this for the copy you send, upload or archive.

A sensible habit is to keep two copies: an editable one for yourself and a flattened one to send. If a form portal rejects the file, try the flattened version, because some systems struggle with live fields.

## What about characters and languages?

Text in fields is written with the form’s font, which covers Latin letters, digits and common symbols. Other alphabets are not available in fields. If a name contains characters the font does not have, the tool tells you instead of writing garbage. For non-Latin text, a flat PDF or a different approach, such as typing the name in Latin letters as the form instructions allow, is usually needed.

## No fields? Type directly onto the page

If the PDF has no fillable fields, for example a scan or a form that is only a picture, the tool switches to a typing mode:

1. Click on the page where a line of text should go.
2. Type the text in the box that appears under the preview.
3. Repeat for every item, on any page.
4. Save the PDF with the text.

The text is written into the page and cannot be edited afterwards, so keep the original. Choose a text size that fits the line, and check the placement on the preview before you save. This mode is also a way to fill XFA forms that cannot be completed with fields, by typing onto their pages.

## After filling

- Look at every page. Fields can run past the edge of a box, and checkboxes on later pages are easy to forget.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Sending the editable version when the recipient needs a final one, or the other way round.
- Typing into a flat scan and assuming the text can be edited later. It cannot.
- Forgetting required fields, marked with an asterisk.
- Missing a checkbox on a later page.
- Not keeping an editable copy for corrections.

## A quick checklist

1. Add the form and look at the field count.
2. Fill every required field and check the optional ones.
3. For a flat form, click and type where each answer goes.
4. Flatten the copy you send, keep the editable copy.
5. Open the result and read it from the first page to the last.

:::faq Frequently asked questions
Q: My PDF shows no fields. Why?
A: The PDF has no real form fields, for example a scan or a form that is only a picture. You can still click the page where text should go, type it and save a copy with the text written on. Forms made with XFA cannot have their fields filled.

Q: What does flattening do?
A: Flattening burns your answers into the page and removes the form fields, so the answers can no longer be edited. Use it for the copy you send and keep an editable copy for yourself.

Q: Can I fill in a signature field?
A: Not with this tool. Use Sign PDF to place a picture of your signature on the page.

Q: Can I use non-Latin characters in the fields?
A: The form font covers Latin letters, digits and common symbols only. If a character cannot be written, the tool tells you.

Q: Can I edit the text I typed onto a flat PDF later?
A: No. It is written into the page. Keep the original file so you can start again.

Q: Is my form uploaded?
A: No. The tool runs inside your browser, so the file stays on your device.
:::

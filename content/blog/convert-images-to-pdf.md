Forms, receipts, homework, ID copies and product photos often arrive as a pile of image files, while the person asking for them wants one PDF. Converting images to PDF is simple, but a few choices decide whether the result looks professional or sloppy: the order of the pages, the page size, the margins and what happens to image quality along the way.

This guide walks through those choices and shows how to build the PDF in your browser, without installing software and without sending your pictures to a server.

:::takeaways
- One tool turns JPG, PNG and mixed images into a single PDF, with one image per page.
- Choose the page size (A4, Letter or Fit to image), the order and the margins before you create the file.
- Photographing paper pages? Scan to PDF adds clean-up that makes text easier to read.
- Resize or compress very large photos first, so the PDF stays small enough to send.
:::

## Pick the right tool for your images

There are three converters, and the right one depends on what you have:

- <tool_jpg_to_pdf>JPG to PDF</tool_jpg_to_pdf> when every file is a JPG. The JPEG data is embedded as it is, without re-compression, so quality does not drop.
- <tool_png_to_pdf>PNG to PDF</tool_png_to_pdf> for screenshots and graphics. Transparency is kept; transparent areas show the white page behind them.
- <tool_images_to_pdf>Images to PDF</tool_images_to_pdf> for a mix. JPG and PNG are embedded directly, and WebP, GIF or BMP files are converted to PNG first if your browser can read them.

## Step by step

1. Add your images. You can drop several at once; each image becomes one page.
2. Put them in order by dragging the rows or using the arrows. The order shown is the page order.
3. Choose a page size: A4, US Letter, or Fit to image, which sizes each page to its picture.
4. Set the orientation and margin. Automatic orientation follows each image, and margins range from none to large.
5. Create the PDF and download it.

## A4, Letter or Fit to image?

Use A4 or US Letter when the PDF will be printed, emailed as a document or uploaded to a portal that expects standard pages. Each image is scaled to fit inside the page, keeps its proportions and is centred, so a wide photo on a portrait page gets bands of white above and below rather than being stretched.

Choose Fit to image when the pictures are the content: a set of screenshots, a comic, a photo album. Every page takes the size of its image, so nothing is scaled or padded and nothing is cropped.

## Getting the order and orientation right

File names rarely sort the way you want, especially when photos come from a phone, so check the list before creating the PDF. If some pictures are sideways, fix them first with the image tools and add them again. With A4 or Letter, Automatic orientation chooses landscape for wide images and portrait for tall ones, which is usually what you want for a mixed set. Pick a fixed orientation only when every page must match.

> Tip: for documents such as receipts, take the photo straight on, in good light and with the page filling the frame. A cleaner picture gives a cleaner PDF, and a scanned page is far easier to read later with <tool_ocr_pdf>OCR PDF</tool_ocr_pdf>.

## Keeping the file size reasonable

A modern phone photo is often 3 to 8 MB, so twenty of them make a PDF too big for most email limits. Because JPEG data is embedded without re-compression, the PDF is about as large as the pictures combined. ## Photographing paper pages: use Scan to PDF

A photo of a printed page is not the same as a scan. It has uneven light, soft shadows from your hand or the phone, and a slightly grey background, and all of that shows up when someone reads or prints the PDF. <tool_scan_to_pdf>Scan to PDF</tool_scan_to_pdf> is built for that case. You add the page photos, put them in order, and choose a clean-up style: Original keeps the colours, Grayscale removes them, and Black and white document evens out shadows and turns the page into dark text on white paper.

The black and white mode suits printed pages and handwritten notes. It is not meant for photographs, where it would throw away tone and detail. Scan to PDF does not recognise the text, so the result is still a set of pictures. If you need to search or copy the words, run <tool_ocr_pdf>OCR PDF</tool_ocr_pdf> afterwards, as described in our guide to <post_ocr-scanned-pdf-searchable>making a scanned PDF searchable</post_ocr-scanned-pdf-searchable>.

On a phone or tablet whose browser supports it, a Take a photo button opens the camera directly. Otherwise choose pictures that are already saved on the device.

## Tips for sharper, better-looking pages

- Photograph pages in daylight or under an even lamp, with the phone directly above the paper instead of at an angle.
- Fill the frame with the page and keep the edges straight; crop out the table around it before building the PDF.
- Hold the camera steady or use a timer, because a slightly blurred photo cannot be repaired afterwards.
- Use the same orientation for every page. If one page is sideways, rotate the picture first so the PDF opens the right way up.
- Name your files in the order you want them, for example page-01, page-02, so the list is already sorted when you add them.

## Limits worth knowing

- Up to 25 MB per image and 100 images per PDF.
- JPG to PDF accepts JPG files only; use Images to PDF for mixed formats.
- Everything runs in your browser, so speed and the number of images you can handle depend on your device’s memory.

:::faq Frequently asked questions
Q: Does converting a JPG to PDF reduce its quality?
A: No. JPG files are embedded in the PDF as they are, without re-compression, so the picture is identical to the original.

Q: Can I put different image formats in one PDF?
A: Yes, with Images to PDF. JPG and PNG are embedded directly, and WebP, GIF and BMP files are converted to PNG first if your browser can decode them.

Q: What is the difference between Images to PDF and Scan to PDF?
A: Images to PDF places your pictures on pages as they are. Scan to PDF does the same and can also clean up photographed paper pages, for example by evening out shadows and turning them into black and white. Neither tool recognises text.

Q: Why does my iPhone photo not appear in the list?
A: Some phone formats, such as HEIC, cannot be read by a browser. Save or export the picture as a JPG first, or change the camera format to the most compatible option, and add it again.

Q: How many images can I put in one PDF?
A: Images to PDF handles many pictures in one go, within the size limits shown on the tool page. If you have far more, build several PDFs and join them with Merge PDF.

Q: Are my pictures uploaded when I convert them?
A: No. The PDF is built inside your browser from files on your device, and nothing is sent to our servers.

:::

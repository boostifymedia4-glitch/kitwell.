A watermark is the faint word or logo that sits across a page: DRAFT on a contract under review, CONFIDENTIAL on an internal report, a company logo on a price list, a name on a document you are sending to a specific person. It tells the reader something about the status of the document without changing the text underneath.

This guide shows how to add a text or image watermark to a PDF in your browser, how to choose size, opacity and angle so the page stays readable, and how to think about what a watermark can and cannot protect.

:::takeaways
- A watermark is drawn on top of the existing page content, with adjustable opacity and angle.
- Use low opacity so the page remains easy to read.
- Text watermarks use Latin letters; for other alphabets, use a transparent PNG image instead.
- A watermark is a signal, not a lock. Anyone with a PDF editor can remove it.
- Combine it with password protection if you need real restrictions.
:::

## What watermarks are good for

Watermarks communicate status and ownership:

- DRAFT or FOR REVIEW tells readers a document is not final.
- CONFIDENTIAL or INTERNAL reminds people not to forward a file.
- COPY marks duplicates of original documents.
- A logo or name makes the source obvious on a handout or price list.
- A recipient’s name on a shared file discourages casual forwarding.

What they do not do is stop determined copying. Treat a watermark as a label, not as security.

## Text or image?

A text watermark is the quickest choice: type the word, pick a size and you are done. Text watermarks use the built-in PDF fonts, which cover Latin letters, digits and common symbols. If your word is in Urdu, Arabic, Chinese or another alphabet, typed text will not work. Instead, create a transparent PNG of the word, or use your logo, and choose the image option. Images can be PNG or JPG, up to 5 MB.

An image watermark is better for logos, company stamps or any design that is more than a word. A PNG with a transparent background keeps the page readable around the shape.

## Opacity: the setting that matters most

If the watermark is too strong, readers cannot read the page; if it is too weak, nobody notices it. Aim for a mark that is clearly visible when you look for it but does not fight with the text:

- 10 to 20 percent opacity works for large diagonal words.
- 30 to 50 percent suits small logos in a corner.
- Dark text on a white page tolerates a stronger mark than a page full of photos.

The watermark sits in front of the page text, so even a light mark covers a little of what is beneath. Test with a dense page, not only with the title page, and zoom in to check that small print is still readable.

## Angle, size and layout

A diagonal word of about 45 degrees across the middle of the page is the classic DRAFT look. It is hard to ignore and does not cover any single line completely. Horizontal marks are more formal and work well as a header, footer or corner logo. A tiled layout repeats the mark across the page, which makes it harder to crop out but also more intrusive.

Choose a size that spans a good part of the page for status words, and a small size for logos. Always preview a representative page.

## Which pages to mark

You can put the watermark on every page or only on a selection. Common patterns:

- Every page for DRAFT or CONFIDENTIAL.
- Only the first page for a logo.
- All pages except the cover for a legal notice.

If you need different marks on different parts of a document, apply them in separate runs, one for each range of pages.

## The limits of a watermark

The watermark is drawn on top of the page. It is not a security feature: anyone with a PDF editor can remove or cover it. This matters in two situations:

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Choosing an opacity so high that the text underneath is hard to read.
- Testing only the cover page, then discovering the mark hides a table on page 12.
- Typing a word in a non-Latin alphabet instead of using an image.
- Treating a watermark as protection for sensitive content.
- Overwriting the original file.

## A quick checklist

1. Decide what the watermark should say: status, owner or recipient.
2. Choose text for a word and an image for a logo or a non-Latin word.
3. Start with low opacity and increase it only if needed.
4. Check a dense page, a photo page and the last page.
5. Keep the original without the watermark.

:::faq Frequently asked questions
Q: Can the watermark be removed again?
A: It is drawn on top of the page and is not a security feature, so anyone with a PDF editor can remove it. For stronger protection, combine it with a password.

Q: Can I use Urdu, Arabic or other alphabets in a text watermark?
A: Not as typed text, because the built-in PDF fonts cover only Latin letters. Make a transparent PNG of your text and use the image option.

Q: Is the watermark in front of or behind the page text?
A: In front. Lower the opacity so the page stays readable.

Q: What image formats are accepted for image watermarks?
A: PNG or JPG, up to 5 MB. A PNG with a transparent background gives the cleanest result.

Q: Can I watermark only some pages?
A: Yes. You can apply the mark to all pages or to a selection.

Q: Is my PDF uploaded?
A: No. The tool runs inside your browser, so the file stays on your device.

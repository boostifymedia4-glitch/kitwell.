The classic routine is silly: print the form, sign it with a pen, scan it again, and email the scan. It wastes paper and time, and the scan often comes out crooked and grey. Placing your signature on the PDF directly is faster and looks cleaner.

But there is something to know before you do it. “Signing a PDF” can mean two different things, and which one you need depends on who asked for the signature. This guide shows how to add a signature in your browser, and explains the difference so you do not send the wrong kind.

:::takeaways
- Sign PDF places a picture of your signature on the pages you choose; you can draw it, type your name or upload an image.
- It is a visual signature: no certificate, no timestamp and no tamper detection.
- Many everyday forms accept it; contracts that require a digital or qualified signature do not.
- The file is processed in your browser and not uploaded; password-protected PDFs must be unlocked first.
:::

## Two kinds of PDF signature

A visual signature is an image of your handwritten signature, or of your typed name, placed on the page. It looks like a signature and is accepted wherever a scanned, hand-signed copy is accepted: many internal forms, school and club documents, delivery receipts, consent slips and simple agreements.

A digital signature is a different technology. It is created with a certificate that identifies you, it records the time, and it makes the document show a warning if anyone changes it afterwards. It is used where the law or a contract requires proof of who signed and that the document did not change.

Sign PDF creates the first kind. It does not create certificates, timestamps or tamper-evident signatures, and it will not fill a signature form field. If the person asking for your signature needs a digital or qualified signature, ask which service or format they accept before you sign.

## Making a signature that looks good

- Draw on a touch screen if you can. A finger or stylus gives a more natural line than a mouse.
- If you upload a picture, sign with a dark pen on plain white paper, take a straight, well-lit photo and crop it tightly. A messy photo leaves grey edges around the signature.
- Keep it a sensible size. A signature that overlaps the printed line or other text looks careless.
- Use the same style each time, so your signature stays recognisable.

## What the signature is, technically

The signature is placed as a picture on top of the page. That has two practical consequences. It does not become part of a signature field in the document, and anyone with the right tools could remove or move it, because nothing protects it. The tool is honest about this limit so that you can choose the right method for important documents.

> Tip: for anything that matters, keep the unsigned original and the signed copy together, and note the date you signed.

## When a visual signature is enough, and when it is not

Usually enough: forms inside a company or school, rental or club applications where a scanned signature is accepted, documents the recipient will print anyway, and receipts or acknowledgements with no legal weight.

Ask first: loans, property documents, contracts of employment, tax or government filings. Some require an electronic signature service or a certificate. Others say explicitly that a scan of a hand-signed page is fine. The sender knows, so ask before you sign.

## Keeping your signature safe

Your signature image is a piece of personal information. Do not leave copies on shared computers, and do not paste it into documents you do not control. This tool does not store your signature, because it never leaves your browser; close the tab and it is gone.

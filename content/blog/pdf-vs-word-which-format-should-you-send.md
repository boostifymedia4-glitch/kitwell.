You have finished a document and you are about to send it. Should it go out as a PDF or as a Word file? The question looks small, but the wrong choice can mean a CV that looks broken on the recruiter's screen, a contract that someone edited by accident or a draft that the other person cannot comment on.

This guide compares the two formats on the points that matter in daily life, gives a simple rule for choosing and shows how to switch from one to the other.

:::takeaways
- Send a PDF when the layout must stay exactly as you made it and the other person only needs to read, print or sign.
- Send a Word file when the other person has to edit, comment or track changes.
- If a recruiter, school or office tells you which format they want, send that one.
- A PDF cannot be edited easily, but that is not the same as security. Use a password if the content is private.
- Turning Word into PDF is simple. Turning a PDF back into a clean Word file is not, so keep your original.
:::

## What each format is

A Word document, with the extension .docx, is a working file. It stores text, styles and settings, and the program on the screen decides how to lay out the pages. Open it on another computer with different fonts or a different version of Word and the page breaks, spacing or fonts can change.

A PDF is a finished page. It stores where every letter, line and picture sits on every page, and it can carry the fonts it needs. Open it on a phone, a laptop or a printer and it looks the same. The price is that it is awkward to edit, because the file describes pages, not paragraphs.

That difference explains almost every point below.

## Layout and appearance

If the look of the page matters, PDF is the safer choice. A CV with a careful layout, a brochure, an invoice, a form or a certificate should arrive the way you designed it.

Word files can shift. A table that fits on one page on your computer can spill onto the next page on someone else's. A font that is installed on your machine but not on theirs is replaced by another font, which changes line lengths and page breaks. These problems are usually small for a plain letter and bigger for designed documents.

## Editing and collaboration

Here Word wins. If your reader has to change the text, add comments, accept or reject tracked changes or reuse your content, send the Word file. A contract draft that goes back and forth between two companies belongs in Word until it is final. A thesis that a supervisor has to mark up belongs in Word too.

A PDF can be commented on and signed with the right tools, but editing its text is awkward and the result is rarely clean. Do not choose PDF for something that has to be worked on.

## Security and control

A PDF is harder to change by accident, and that is often what people mean when they say that PDF is safer. It is not the same as being secure.

- A PDF is not protected against changes unless you add protection, and anyone with the right program can edit a PDF.
- A Word file can be edited by the recipient, who might change a line or delete a section without noticing.

## File size

There is no general winner. A text-only Word file can be very small, and a PDF made from it is usually larger because it carries fonts. A document full of pictures can be large in both formats.

## Compatibility and accessibility

PDF readers exist on every phone, computer and many TVs, and browsers open PDFs without any extra program. Word files need Word or a compatible program. Most devices can open them, but the look may differ.

For people who use screen readers, both formats can be made accessible, but only if the document is built with real headings and lists. A PDF made from a well-structured Word file keeps much of that structure, and a PDF made from scanned pictures has no readable text at all. ## Which one for which job

A simple rule works for most cases: follow the instructions of the person who will receive the file, and when there are none, choose by what the reader has to do.

- A CV or application: follow the employer's instructions. If they do not say, a PDF keeps your layout, and a clean single-column design with plain text works best, since some systems read CVs automatically and handle simple files better than designed ones. Keep a Word copy ready in case they ask.
- A contract that is final: PDF. A contract that is still being negotiated: Word.
- An invoice, receipt or certificate: PDF.
- A form that has to be filled in: a PDF form, or Word if the other side will type into it. Our guide to <post_how-to-fill-out-a-pdf-form>filling out a PDF form</post_how-to-fill-out-a-pdf-form> shows how.
- A draft that others will edit: Word.
- A document that people will read on a phone: PDF.

## Before you send: a short checklist

1. Decide what the reader has to do with the file: read, print, sign or edit.
2. Check the instructions of the recipient.
3. Look at the finished file on another device if you can.
4. Read the properties of the PDF, such as the title and author. <tool_edit_pdf_metadata>Edit PDF Metadata</tool_edit_pdf_metadata> lets you correct them.
5. Protect private files with a password, and keep your original.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Sending a designed Word file and hoping that it looks the same everywhere.
- Sending a PDF for something that has to be edited.
- Believing that a PDF is secure just because it is a PDF.
- Deleting the Word original after exporting.
- Sending a PDF made from scanned pictures to someone who needs to search it.
- Forgetting the file name, the title and the author fields.

:::faq Frequently asked questions
Q: Is a PDF or a Word file better for a CV?
A: Follow the employer's instructions first. If they do not say, a PDF keeps your layout, and a simple single-column design with plain text is the safest. Keep a Word copy ready in case they ask for it.

Q: Can I edit a PDF?
A: Some programs can, but it is awkward and the result is rarely clean. If you need to edit, keep and edit the original Word file.

Q: Is a PDF more secure than a Word file?
A: Not by itself. A PDF is harder to change by accident, but anyone with the right program can edit it. Use a password to protect private files.

Q: Does Kitwell convert PDF to Word?
A: No. You can extract the text or convert the PDF to Markdown, and then paste the result into a new document.

Q: Why does my Word file look different on another computer?
A: The other computer may have different fonts or a different version of Word, which changes line lengths and page breaks. A PDF avoids this.
:::

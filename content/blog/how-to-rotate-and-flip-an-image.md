A picture that is the wrong way round is one of the most common small problems with photos. A scan comes out sideways, a phone photo opens upside down in one program and correctly in another, a horizon is slightly crooked, or a product shot needs to face the other way to match the rest of a page. None of these should need a large editing program.

This guide shows how to turn and mirror pictures in your browser, explains what happens at the corners when you rotate by an odd angle, tells you when flipping helps and when it causes trouble and covers the quality questions people ask most.

:::takeaways
- Quarter turns of 90, 180 and 270 degrees keep every pixel and the original size. Custom angles make the canvas larger.
- On a custom angle, the empty corners are filled with a background colour in JPG and stay transparent in PNG and WebP.
- A horizontal flip mirrors left and right. A vertical flip turns the picture upside down.
- Do not flip pictures that contain text, signs or logos, because they will read backwards.
- Your original file is not changed, and you can process up to 20 pictures at once.
:::

## Why pictures end up the wrong way

There are a few usual causes.

- Scanners and phone scanning apps sometimes save a page sideways.
- Phones record how they were held when the photo was taken, and store that as a small note inside the file. Some programs follow the note and others ignore it, so the same photo can look right in one place and sideways in another.
- Cameras and phones are held at slight angles, which leaves a tilted horizon.
- Front cameras can produce mirrored pictures, so text on a sign looks backwards.

Whatever the cause, the cure is the same: turn or mirror the picture, save it as a new file and use that copy. When the new picture is saved, it is written the way it looks, so it appears the same in every program.

## Quarter turns

<tool_image_rotator>Rotate Image</tool_image_rotator> turns pictures by 90, 180 or 270 degrees, or by any angle you type. The quarter turns are the cleanest. They move every pixel to its new place, nothing is blended and nothing is lost. A 90 degree turn swaps the width and the height, so a picture of 4000 by 3000 pixels becomes 3000 by 4000.

Which way is which? A turn of 90 degrees goes clockwise, 180 is upside down and 270 is the same as a quarter turn anticlockwise. If you are not sure, apply a turn and look at the preview, then try the other direction.

## Custom angles and the corners

Sometimes the picture is only a little crooked. You can type a custom angle, for example 2 or minus 1.5 degrees, to level a horizon. Because a tilted rectangle does not fit into a straight frame, the tool makes the canvas larger so that nothing is cut off. The result has empty triangles in the corners.

What fills them depends on the format.

- In JPG, which cannot store transparency, the corners are filled with the background colour that you choose.
- In PNG and WebP, the corners stay transparent.

If you do not want blank corners, you can crop them away afterwards with <tool_image_cropper>Crop Image</tool_image_cropper>, and our guide to <post_how-to-crop-an-image-to-a-specific-size>cropping an image to a specific size</post_how-to-crop-an-image-to-a-specific-size> explains how to do it precisely. <tool_photo_editor>Photo Editor</tool_photo_editor> offers a straighten control that zooms in a little so no empty corners appear at all, which is quicker for levelling a horizon.

A custom angle blends pixels, so it is slightly softer than a quarter turn. For tilts of a degree or two, the loss is small and usually invisible.

## Flipping and mirroring

<tool_image_flipper>Flip Image</tool_image_flipper> mirrors a picture. You choose a horizontal or a vertical flip.

- A horizontal flip mirrors left and right, like a mirror. A person who looked to the left now looks to the right.
- A vertical flip turns the picture upside down along its horizontal axis, so the top becomes the bottom, without swapping left and right.
- Doing both, one after the other, is the same as turning the picture by 180 degrees, which the rotate tool does in one step.

People flip pictures for several reasons: to make a subject face into the page in a layout, to fix a selfie that was saved mirrored, to create a symmetrical design or to check how an image looks from the other side.

## When not to flip

Flipping has one big trap: text. A picture that contains a sign, a label, a screenshot or a logo reads backwards after a horizontal flip. Many brands also have a clear left-right direction, and flipping their logo changes it. Maps, clocks and charts can mislead too.

Faces look slightly different when flipped, because nobody's face is perfectly symmetrical. For portraits that matter, such as a profile picture, keep the original orientation.

Never flip a document that people have to read or an official picture such as an ID photo.

## Keeping quality

A few habits keep pictures looking their best.

- Prefer quarter turns where you can. They do not blend pixels.
- Rotate once. Turning a picture several times in small steps blurs it more each time. Work out the full angle first.
- Rotate before you resize or compress, so the later steps work on the correct shape. <tool_image_resizer>Resize Image</tool_image_resizer> and <tool_image_compressor>Compress Image</tool_image_compressor> come after.
- Choose PNG if you will edit the picture again, and JPG or WebP for the final, smaller version.

Animated pictures use only their first frame, and the original file is never changed. Details such as the location and the camera model are not carried into the new picture.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Flipping a picture with text and finding it backwards.
- Rotating a JPG by an odd angle and being surprised by the filled corners.
- Rotating the same picture several times in small steps.
- Forgetting that a quarter turn swaps width and height.
- Resizing before rotating.
- Overwriting the original instead of saving a new file.

## A quick checklist

1. Decide whether you need a quarter turn, a small custom angle or a flip.
2. Use a quarter turn when possible, and one custom angle at a time.
3. Check the corners, and crop them away if needed.
4. Check that no text is mirrored.
5. Resize and compress after the picture is in the right orientation.

A: The canvas grows to fit the rotated image. For JPG output the empty corners are filled with your chosen background. PNG and WebP keep them transparent.

Q: What is the difference between a horizontal and a vertical flip?
A: A horizontal flip mirrors left and right, like a mirror. A vertical flip turns the image upside down along its horizontal axis.

A: Yes. You can add up to 20 files per batch, each up to 25 MB.

Q: Does rotating lose quality?
A: Quarter turns keep every pixel. A custom angle blends pixels slightly, so a very small tilt is nearly invisible.

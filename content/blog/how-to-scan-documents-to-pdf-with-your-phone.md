A phone camera is a good enough scanner for most paper: a signed form, a receipt, an ID copy for a bank, a page of lecture notes or a contract that has to be sent today. The problem is that photos of pages are a mess to send. They are many separate pictures, with shadows, a visible table and tilted edges, and the receiver has to open each one.

A single clean PDF solves this. This guide shows how to turn photos of paper pages into one PDF in your browser, how to take pictures that scan well, what the three clean-up styles do and how to finish with a small, searchable file.

:::takeaways
- Scan to PDF turns photos or scans of pages into one PDF with the page size, margin and clean-up style that you choose.
- The Black and white document style evens out shadows and suits printed pages and notes, but not photographs.
- The pages are pictures. The text is not recognised, so run the result through OCR to make it searchable.
- Each image can be up to 25 MB, and a PDF can have up to 100 pages.
- Photos in formats that browsers cannot read, such as HEIC, must be saved as JPG first.
:::

## Why scan to PDF

A PDF is the format that everyone can open and nobody can easily break. Forms, banks, schools and offices ask for it. It keeps pages together in order, it looks the same on every device and it can be signed, merged and compressed with the right tools.

Photos have none of that. A set of five pictures in a chat can arrive in the wrong order, in different orientations and with sizes that are too big to attach. One PDF is easier for everybody, including you.

## How to take good pictures of paper

The quality of the result depends mostly on the photo. A few habits make a big difference.

1. Use daylight or a bright lamp that does not cast your own shadow on the page. Avoid the camera flash, which creates a bright spot.
2. Put the page on a flat, plain surface in a contrasting colour, such as a white sheet on a dark table.
3. Hold the phone straight above the page, parallel to it, so the edges do not lean.
4. Fill the frame with the page, but keep all four corners in view.
5. Wait for the focus before you shoot, and keep your hands steady.
6. Check that the text is sharp when you zoom in, and retake the picture if it is not.

Scanning apps often crop automatically. Here you can crop the picture first with any tool, or simply leave a thin border. The page will fit once you choose the page size.

## How to scan to PDF

<tool_scan_to_pdf>Scan to PDF</tool_scan_to_pdf> runs inside your browser tab, so your pictures stay on your device.

1. Open the tool and add the photos or scans of your pages. On a phone or tablet whose browser supports it, the Take a photo button opens the camera directly, and otherwise you choose pictures that are saved on the device.
2. Put the pages in order, and choose the page size and a clean-up style.
3. Create the PDF and download it.

Each image can be up to 25 MB, and you can add up to 100 pages.

## Page size, orientation and margin

The tool offers a few settings that decide how each picture sits on a page.

- Page size: A4, US Letter or Fit to image. A4 and Letter give standard pages that print and send well. Fit to image makes each page exactly as large as its picture, with no scaling or white edges, which is useful for receipts and odd-shaped papers.
- Orientation: automatic, portrait or landscape. Automatic follows each picture. The setting does not apply when the page fits the image.
- Margin: none, small or medium. A margin leaves white space around the picture, which looks tidier and avoids cutting off edges when the page is printed.

Large photos are scaled down to about A4 size at 300 dots per inch, which keeps the PDF small without hurting readability. ## The three clean-up styles

Clean-up is what turns a photograph of a page into something that looks scanned.

- Original keeps the colours exactly. Use it for coloured forms, stamps, signatures in ink and photographs.
- Grayscale removes colour. It makes the file a little smaller and looks neutral, which suits many documents.
- Black and white document turns the page into pure black text on white paper and evens out shadows and uneven lighting. It suits printed pages and notes best. It is not suitable for photographs, drawings with light colours or anything where grey shades matter, because they turn into black or white.

If a page has a shadow across it, Black and white document usually fixes it. If it removes faint pencil marks or light stamps, switch to Grayscale or Original for that page.

## Make the scan searchable

The pages in the PDF are pictures. The tool does not recognise the text, so you cannot search the file or copy words from it. To fix that, run the result through <tool_ocr_pdf>OCR PDF</tool_ocr_pdf>, which adds an invisible text layer to the pages. ## Formats and picture problems

Some phones save photos as HEIC, a format that browsers cannot read. If a picture is not accepted, save or export it as JPG first, or change the camera setting to the most compatible format. If your pictures are PNG screenshots, our guide to <post_how-to-convert-png-to-jpg>converting PNG to JPG</post_how-to-convert-png-to-jpg> explains what that conversion does.

## Privacy and sensitive documents

Documents such as IDs, bank letters and contracts deserve care. The pictures stay on your device while you work, but the file you create can travel far. Before you send a scan, think about who will receive it, send it only through a channel that you trust and delete copies you no longer need. If a page contains something that must not be seen, hide it before you share the file. Our guide to <post_how-to-redact-a-pdf-properly>redacting a PDF properly</post_how-to-redact-a-pdf-properly> explains how.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Taking photos with a shadow across the page, or with the flash on.
- Using Black and white document for pictures where shades of grey matter.
- Forgetting to check the order of the pages.
- Sending a very large scan by email without compressing it.
- Expecting the text in the scan to be searchable without OCR.
- Sending an ID scan without thinking about who will receive it.

## A quick checklist

1. Take sharp, straight photos in good light.
2. Add the pages, put them in order and choose the page size and the clean-up style.
3. Create the PDF and check every page.
4. Run OCR if you need to search or copy the text.
5. Compress the file if it is too large, then send it carefully.

Almost every PDF needs a little tidying at some point. A scan has a blank page in the middle. A report contains a cover sheet that the client should not see. Two chapters are in the wrong order. A contract has a page from an older version that must go before you send it. You do not need an expensive program to fix any of this.

This guide shows how to remove pages and rearrange them in your browser with a thumbnail view, how to do it with the mouse and with the keyboard, and which checks to make before the file leaves your hands.

:::takeaways
- You see every page as a thumbnail, so you can remove or move pages by looking at them.
- You can delete pages by clicking them, or by typing a range such as 2, 5-7.
- Pages can be reordered with drag and drop or with move-earlier and move-later buttons.
- Your original file is never changed. You get a new PDF.
- At least one page must remain in the result.
:::

## Two tools for two jobs

Two tools cover most needs, and it helps to know which to pick.

- <tool_remove_pdf_pages>Remove PDF Pages</tool_remove_pdf_pages> is made for deleting. You mark the pages you do not want, and everything else is kept in the original order.
- <tool_reorder_pdf_pages>Reorder PDF Pages</tool_reorder_pdf_pages> is made for rearranging. You drag pages into a new order, and you can also remove or rotate pages in the same view.

If you need only a few of many pages, <tool_extract_pdf_pages>Extract PDF Pages</tool_extract_pdf_pages> is the opposite approach: you choose the pages to keep. Our guide to <post_how-to-extract-pages-from-a-pdf>extracting pages from a PDF</post_how-to-extract-pages-from-a-pdf> explains it.

## How to remove pages

The tool works inside your browser tab, so the file stays on your device.

1. Open the tool and add a PDF. Its pages appear as thumbnails. The limit is 100 MB per file.
2. Click the pages you want to delete, or type a range in the box, such as 2, 5-7. That removes page 2 and pages 5, 6 and 7.
3. Save the new PDF.

Page numbers refer to the position in the original file, counting from 1. If you type a range, check the thumbnails once more. They show exactly which pages are marked, so a mistake in the numbers is easy to see.

The tool does not let you remove every page. At least one page has to stay, since a PDF without pages does not exist.

## How to reorder pages

1. Open <tool_reorder_pdf_pages>Reorder PDF Pages</tool_reorder_pdf_pages> and add your PDF. The pages appear as thumbnails.
2. Drag pages to change their order, or use the arrow buttons on each page to move it earlier or later.
3. Remove pages you do not need, and rotate pages that are sideways.
4. Save the reordered PDF.

The buttons are useful for more than comfort. They let you work with the keyboard alone, which is easier for people who cannot use a mouse, and they give you precise control on a phone, where dragging a small thumbnail can be awkward.

For a long document, work in steps. Move one chapter at a time, look at the thumbnails after each move and do not try to rearrange thirty pages in one go.

## Common situations and the right approach

A few jobs come up again and again.

- A blank page in a scan. Remove it. If your scanner often inserts blank pages, check the file once before you send it.
- A cover sheet or a fax page. Remove it, and check whether the page numbers inside the document still make sense.
- Two documents that must be combined first. Merge them, then reorder. Our guide to <post_how-to-merge-pdf-files>merging PDF files</post_how-to-merge-pdf-files> covers that part, and <tool_merge_pdf>Merge PDF</tool_merge_pdf> does it.
- One large file that should become several. Our guide to <post_how-to-split-a-pdf>splitting a PDF</post_how-to-split-a-pdf> explains it.
- Pages that are sideways. Rotate them in the same view, or use <tool_rotate_pdf>Rotate PDF</tool_rotate_pdf> for the whole file. Our guide to <post_how-to-rotate-pdf-pages-permanently>rotating PDF pages permanently</post_how-to-rotate-pdf-pages-permanently> explains how to make the rotation stick. ## Page numbers and references

Two things can go wrong after you change the page order.

First, page numbers that are printed on the pages do not change. If page 3 was moved to the end, it still says 3. If the document needs numbers, remove the old ones and add new ones after the last change. <tool_add_page_numbers>Add Page Numbers</tool_add_page_numbers> numbers every page in the order of the final file, and our guide to <post_how-to-add-page-numbers-to-a-pdf>adding page numbers to a PDF</post_how-to-add-page-numbers-to-a-pdf> explains the options.

Second, references inside the text, such as see page 12, point to the old numbers. Read the places where the text mentions pages, and fix them in the source document if you can.

A table of contents has the same problem. Links in the contents may still work, since they point to pages and not to numbers, but the numbers that people read can be wrong.

## Check before you share

After saving, open the new file and look at it page by page.

1. Count the pages. Is the total what you expected?
2. Check the first and last pages, because those are the ones that people notice.
3. Check the pages around every change.
4. Read the properties of the new file. Old metadata can travel with the document. Our guide to <post_how-to-view-pdf-properties-and-metadata>viewing PDF properties and metadata</post_how-to-view-pdf-properties-and-metadata> explains what to look at.

Do not use page removal as a way to hide sensitive content inside a page. Removing a whole page leaves the other pages as they were. If you need to black out a name or a number on a page you want to keep, use a redaction tool. Our guide to <post_how-to-redact-a-pdf-properly>redacting a PDF properly</post_how-to-redact-a-pdf-properly> explains the difference.

## Password-protected files

If your PDF is protected with a password, the page tools cannot open it, and you will see a message. Unlock the file first and then organise it. Our guide to <post_password-protect-and-unlock-a-pdf>protecting and unlocking a PDF</post_password-protect-and-unlock-a-pdf> explains how.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Typing page numbers from the printed numbers instead of the position in the file.
- Forgetting that printed page numbers and references do not change.
- Rearranging a very long document in one step, then losing track of what moved.
- Saving over the only copy. Keep the original, and give the new file a clear name.
- Removing a page when you needed to redact a few words on it.
- Sharing the new file without checking its properties.

## A quick checklist

1. Add the file and look at the thumbnails.
2. Mark the pages to remove, or drag pages into the new order.
3. Save, then open the new file and check the page count and the first and last pages.
4. Renumber pages and fix references if the document needs them.
5. Read the properties before you share the file.

:::faq Frequently asked questions
Q: Does it change my original file?
A: No. You get a new PDF without the selected pages. Your original stays as it was.

Q: Can I remove every page?
A: No. At least one page has to stay.

Q: Can I reorder with the keyboard?
A: Yes. Use the move-earlier and move-later buttons on each page.

Q: How do I type which pages to delete?
A: Type a range such as 2, 5-7, or click the thumbnails. Page numbers count from 1 in the original file.

Q: What if my PDF has a password?
A: Unlock it first. Password-protected PDFs cannot be organised until they are unlocked.
:::
